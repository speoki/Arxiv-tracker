Retrieval Over Training: Similarity search-based Model Selection for Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Christos Panourgias, Roberto Stanzione, Adrien Petralia, Themis Palpanas, Paul Boniol
First: 2026-10-14T14:18:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T11:07:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Anomaly detection is a fundamental task for time-series analytics, with important implications for the downstream performance of many applications. Despite the large number of anomaly detection methods proposed in the literature, recent benchmark studies have shown that no single detector performs best across highly heterogeneous time series. Therefore, a practical and scalable solution is to develop a model-selection method that, for a given time series, selects the anomaly detector most likely to perform well. Nevertheless, the model selection approaches proposed in the literature suffer from a significant drop in accuracy when applied in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings. In this paper, we tackle the aforementioned limitation and propose RAMSAD, a retrieval-based framework for model selection in time-series anomaly detection. Given a new time series, our method queries a knowledge base of previously observed instances, retrieves the top-k most similar series, and transfers detector recommendations from their performance profiles. The framework can operate with standard similarity measures as well as embedding-based representations. Our results show that RAMSAD outperforms existing baselines in In-Distribution (ID) settings and is the most robust selector in OOD, where, when selecting a small subset of detectors, it matches or exceeds ensembles that run the entire detector pool, at a fraction of their cost. Overall, we demonstrate that similarity-based retrieval constitutes a strong and efficient foundation for model selection in time-series anomaly detection.

Abstract
Many complex systems combine dense background structure with sparse contextual information. We introduce a diffusion-based framework for analyzing how sparse condition-specific layers reshape diffusion geometry in multilayer hypergraphs. Each layer is represented as a weighted hypergraph, layers are coupled through shared entities, and random walks on the coupled system induce multiscale diffusion distances between nodes. We apply the framework to disease-conditioned gene networks by coupling a dense MSigDB functional gene-set layer to sparse disease-specific DGIdb drug-gene hypergraphs, with disease-associated drugs selected from DDDB and HumanNet-GSP used to define external gene weights. Across Bipolar Disorder, Schizophrenia, Leukemia, and Breast Cancer, the disease-specific layer contains less than 2 percent of genes in the coupled system, yet substantially changes diffusion distances and community structure. Centrality analysis suggests that this disproportionate effect arises because DGIdb-associated genes occupy influential positions in the MSigDB-derived functional network. The resulting diffusion-derived communities are stable under subsampling and show coherent post hoc functional enrichment, including signaling and neurotransmission categories in neuropsychiatric diseases and immune, translational, and metabolic categories in cancer-associated diseases. Community-level comparisons further reveal disease similarities not reducible to direct DGIdb gene overlap, including a Breast Cancer-Schizophrenia relationship consistent with recent biomedical evidence. These results show that sparse contextual layers can induce interpretable nonlocal changes in higher-order network geometry.

Abstract
Learning informative representations of mechanical Triply Periodic Minimal Surface (TPMS) metamaterials is important for mechanics analysis and structural design, yet existing methods have rarely explicitly supervised learned representations with information about global structural connectivity that is relevant to mechanical response. We propose a topology-aware representation learning framework that integrates persistent homology into a DMAE backbone to explicitly encode global structural connectivity. A persistence-guided masking strategy prioritizes patches associated with persistent topological features, while an auxiliary persistent-homology decoder encourages the latent representation to retain information about topological feature counts across connected components, tunnels, and voids. We evaluate the learned representations on two complementary TPMS datasets using family classification, mechanical-property classification, unsupervised clustering, and regression. Across the two datasets, our method demonstrates strong performance in both structural family and mechanical-property classification, improving family classification by up to 3.9% while achieving the best linear-probe performance. More notably, unsupervised property-clustering performance improves by up to 26.7% and 51.5% across the two datasets, while strong regression performance is retained with $R^2=0.993$. These results establish persistent topology as an effective inductive bias for representation learning of complex three-dimensional mechanical metamaterials.

Tracking a Small Set of Experts in Online Convex Optimization
Authors: Angelos Assos, Anand Brahmbhatt, Elad Hazan
First: 2026-10-14T16:13:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:13:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
For the fundamental problem of prediction from expert advice, Freund asked whether one could compete with a comparator that switches $k$ times among only $m$ experts. For a discrete set of experts, this problem was resolved by Bousquet and Warmuth (2020), who gave an efficient algorithm with optimal regret matching the information-theoretic limit. We study the analogous problem in online convex optimization, where the action space is continuous. An inefficient algorithm can be derived by discretizing the action space and applying the Bousquet--Warmuth algorithm, giving regret of $\widetilde{O}(md+k)$, while the best known efficient algorithm for this problem has regret $\widetilde{O}(dk)$. We show that this computational inefficiency is inherent, establishing a separation between the information-theoretic and polynomial-time regret achievable for this problem. Specifically, under the hypergraphic planted-clique conjecture, we show that for every polynomial-time algorithm there exist instances for which it incurs at least $(k\sqrt d)^{1-o(1)}$ regret.

Abstract
When does a human correction become useful learning, and what establishes better segmentation at matched human effort? This critical narrative review follows feedback through its selection, learner update, and evaluation. It compares active learning, machine teaching, interactive segmentation, and sequential predictive evaluation using a common mathematical account of target information, predictive error, response predictability, and human work. An exact Bayesian update converts evaluation-label information into expected log-loss improvement; an excess-risk decomposition explains what an approximate learner can lose. Abstract
Vision-language models have shown strong performance, but they often generalize poorly to specialized domains. Abstract
Anomaly detection in multivariate time series is a central challenge in industrial monitoring, as failures frequently arise from complex temporal dynamics and cross-sensor interactions. While recent deep learning models, including graph neural networks and Transformers, have demonstrated strong empirical performance, most approaches remain primarily correlational and offer limited support for causal interpretation and root-cause localization. This study introduces a causally-constrained probabilistic forecasting framework which is a Causally Guided Transformer (CGT) model for multivariate time-series anomaly detection, integrating an explicit time-lagged causal graph prior with deep sequence modeling. For each target variable, a dedicated forecasting block employs a hard parent mask derived from causal discovery to restrict the main prediction pathway to graph-supported causes, while a latent Gaussian head captures predictive uncertainty. To leverage residual correlational information without compromising the causal representation, a shadow auxiliary path with stop-gradient isolation and a safety-gated blending mechanism is incorporated to suppress non-causal contributions when reliability is low. These findings suggest that causal structural priors can improve both robustness and interpretability in detecting deep anomalies in multivariate sensor systems.

Abstract
Many complex systems involve group interactions, which can readily be represented by higher-order structures such as hypergraphs. However, when observing a system's dynamical evolution, establishing whether that evolution actually results from higher-order interactions, or whether pairwise interactions suffice to explain the data, remains a challenge. Even in the simple case of a temporal sequence of nodes visited during a diffusion process, such a task is intricate. Indeed, group interactions leave their traces as memory on the observed nodes, while path dependence in a purely pairwise process can mimic that same footprint without any group structure. To resolve this, we propose an inference pipeline based on a hidden Markov model (HMM) that tests, directly from an observed sequence of nodes carrying no group labels, whether higher-order interactions are required to describe the dynamics. The HMM latent states represent higher-order interactions, seen as hyperedges of an unobserved hypergraph, so that their detection becomes a model selection problem between the HMM class and a class of null models without higher-order interactions. We first validate this approach on synthetic node sequences generated from random walks on graphs and hypergraphs, showing that it recovers both the number and the composition of the underlying hyperedges, while correctly rejecting them when the generating process is pairwise. As a second step, we consider a validation based on empirical data, namely empirical trajectories of ball possession in football matches. This application shows that the pipeline goes beyond providing statistical evidence of the presence of higher-order interactions, and yields interpretable underlying structures: the recovered hyperedges map directly onto recognizable tactical team formations.

Abstract
Persistent homology summarizes the topological evolution of a graph as persistence diagrams (PDs). Generating graphs whose topology matches a target descriptor is increasingly important, yet existing topology-aware generative models rely on soft regularization, producing graphs whose PDs may only be approximately similar. We initiate a principled investigation of the generation problem: produce graphs that realize a target PD exactly. Abstract
Impact-echo testing is a nondestructive testing method used to detect internal defects in concrete structures. However, boundary reflections, structured interference, and measurement noise can produce complex Fourier amplitude spectra, making defect classification difficult. We constructed a two-dimensional frequency--amplitude persistence image (FA-PI) from the Fourier amplitude spectrum. Using a fixed parameterization of exact multi-parameter persistent homology, each retained Fourier component is represented with frequency-bin location as one coordinate and amplitude as the other, and Gaussian kernels are then applied in this two-dimensional space. This construction has a direct analytical connection to the corresponding one-dimensional Gaussian-smoothed Fourier amplitude spectrum. FA-PI was evaluated on an experimental backfill-void dataset and a public bridge-deck impact-echo dataset, and compared with raw and Gaussian-smoothed Fourier amplitude spectra using a linear classifier with a Softmax output and identical signal-level data splits. Performance was assessed using accuracy and macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score. Across the two datasets, FA-PI consistently outperformed the raw and Gaussian-smoothed Fourier baselines in the noisy-condition summaries. These findings indicate that representing Fourier spectral components in a two-dimensional frequency--amplitude space can improve impact-echo defect classification beyond frequency-axis smoothing alone.

Abstract
Multiplayer social dilemmas on hypergraphs show a variety of emergent phenomena, which has been attributed to group interactions. Here we test whether pairwise dynamics can reproduce them as well. We derive the mesoscopic Langevin equation of a testbed model and find the operator content of absorbing phase transitions: a directed-percolation line, a tricritical point, and a first-order region whose spinodal is the explosive transition. For these games, the explosive transition and the critical mass therefore do not require group interactions, and the critical behavior is that of the pairwise theory.

Abstract
In traditional models of behavioral and opinion dynamics on networks, researchers suppose that interactions occur between pairs of individuals. However, in reality, social interactions also occur in sets of three or more individuals. A common way to incorporate such polyadic interactions is to study dynamical processes on hypergraphs, in which interactions can occur between any number of the individuals. A major current effort is to generalize popular dynamical processes from graphs to hypergraphs. One important model is the Watts threshold model (WTM), which describes a simplistic social spreading process and was extended recently by Chen et al. from dyadic networks (i.e., ordinary graphs) to polyadic networks (i.e., hypergraphs). In the present paper, we extend their discrete-time model to continuous time by using approximate master equations (AMEs). By employing AMEs, we are able to very accurately model the mean behavior of the continuous-time system. We reduce the high-dimensional AME system to a system of three coupled ordinary differential equations without any detectable loss of accuracy. This much lower-dimensional system is computationally faster to solve numerically, and it is also easier to interpret. We linearize the reduced AME system and derive a cascade condition, which allows us to determine when a large spreading event occurs. We then examine our reduced AME system on a contact hypergraph of a French primary school and on a hypergraph of computer-science coauthorships. We find that our full and reduced AME systems give reasonably good descriptions of the mean time-dependent fraction of active nodes in the polyadic WTM dynamics on these empirical networks.

First: 2026-07-18T20:22:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T23:13:31+00:00
Comments: 73 pages, 25 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Persistence diagrams are fundamental descriptors in topological data analysis. Many statistical and machine learning methods require persistence diagrams to be mapped into vector spaces or compared through kernels. Although many persistence diagram vectorizations and persistence kernels have been proposed, there is no general framework for determining which persistence diagram vectorizations are synthesizable. Spectral synthesis asks whether the exponential monomials contained in the translation-invariant variety generated by a function span a dense subspace of that variety. We introduce uniformly discrete spectral synthesis for persistence diagram vectorizations using Fourier analysis on groups of virtual persistence diagrams. When the space of persistence intervals is separable, we prove sufficient conditions for uniformly discrete spectral synthesis and derive approximation results for virtual persistence diagrams with bounded total multiplicity. We then classify many persistence diagram vectorizations and persistence kernels from the literature by uniformly discrete spectral synthesis.

Persistence diagrams are fundamental descriptors in topological data analysis.
The rational homology of independence complexes of Kneser graphs on triples
Authors: Ziqin Feng
First: 2026-10-12T20:59:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T20:59:54+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The independence complex of the Kneser graph $\KG(n,k)$ is the simplicial complex of pairwise intersecting families of $n$-subsets of $[2n+k]$; equivalently, it is a Vietoris--Rips complex of the $n$-subsets of $[2n+k]$ under the symmetric-difference metric. Feng and Wang asked whether, for $n=3$, its reduced homology is nontrivial only in dimensions $6$ and $9$, and conjectured the rank in dimension $9$. We answer the question affirmatively over the rationals and determine all the associated Betti numbers. For every $k\ge0$, the only nonzero reduced Betti numbers using rationals of $\Ind(\KG(3,k))$ are \[ β_6=\binom{k+5}{7}+29\binom{k+6}{7} \qquad\text{and}\qquad β_9=\binom{k+6}{6}. \] The proof uses an inductive decomposition based on a simplicial retraction and Barmak's star clusters, Dowker duality together with Jonsson's computation of the homology of complexes of graphs with bounded covering number, and a relative Mayer--Vietoris spectral sequence which reduces the problem to the six-element sets.

Signature-Kernel One-Class Learning with Manifold Regularisation for Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Shervin Rahimzadeh Arashloo
First: 2025-07-31T11:27:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T14:12:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
One-class learning is an effective methodology for time series anomaly detection, yet kernel one-class approaches often compare sequences in a pointwise fashion and ignore the geometry of normal data. In this work, we present a manifold-regularised, large-margin $\ell_p$-support vector data description ($\ell_p$-SVDD) method that analyses sequences as paths via the signature kernel. By incorporating a a graph-Laplacian penalty into the $\ell_p$-SVDD objective function and utilising the representer theorem for manifold regularisation, we illustrate that the dual task retains the functional form of the original one with a modified kernel matrix. As such, existing Frank--Wolfe solvers can be applied unchanged. We show that this new modified kernel is a valid kernel matrix and under a generic norm bound on the discriminant, the proposed model yields a smaller Rademacher-complexity bound than the baseline. Furthermore, the signature kernel which is computed through a Goursat partial differential equation and can handle irregularly sampled data and is invariant to reparametrisation has not previously been used for one-class time series anomaly detection to the best of our knowledge. Our experiments on five widely used benchmarks, evaluated without point adjustment, demonstrate that the proposed approach compares very favourably with the state of the art approaches.

One-class learning is an effective methodology for time series anomaly detection, yet kernel one-class approaches often compare sequences in a pointwise fashion and ignore the geometry of normal data.
The Geometry of the Set of Equivalent Linear Neural Networks
Authors: Jonathan Richard Shewchuk, Sagnik Bhattacharya
First: 2024-04-23T09:20:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T09:52:09+00:00
Comments: 135 pages, 25 figures, 7 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We characterize the geometry of the set of all weight vectors for which a fully-connected linear neural network computes the same linear transformation $W$. Said another way, the set of all sequences of matrices (of predetermined sizes) whose product is $W$. This set of weight vectors is an algebraic variety in the Euclidean weight space, called the fiber of $W$ under the matrix multiplication map. Sometimes this variety is a manifold, but usually not. The rank stratification of a fiber is a natural partition of the fiber into smooth manifolds of various dimensions called strata. We derive the dimensions of these strata and characterize how they are connected to each other. They satisfy the frontier condition: if a stratum intersects the closure of another stratum, then the former stratum is a subset of the closure of the latter stratum. This subset relationship can be expressed as a partial order with a single minimal element, a stratum that is included in the closure of every other stratum. Hence the strata can be arranged in a hierarchy represented by a directed acyclic graph that represents that partial order; we give an algorithm that computes that graph. A key result describes the relationship between this partial order and the ranks of certain matrices in the network. We derive the tangent and normal subspaces of the strata, and we construct elegant bases for those subspaces. Each stratum represents a different pattern of information flow through the network, expressed as a barcode, borrowed from the study of persistent homology. The topology of a stratum depends solely on its barcode. So does its geometry, up to a linear transformation in weight space. Connections among the strata are best understood through "barcode moves," which can be expressed as simple modifications of the barcodes.
Summary / 总结
We characterize the geometry of the set of all weight vectors for which a fully-connected linear neural network computes the same linear transformation $W$.

OneShot: Label-Efficient Detector Weighting for Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Seunghan Lee, Jun Seo, Jaehoon Lee, Junhyeok Kang, Sangjun Han, Sungdong Yoo, Minjae Kim, Tae Yoon Lim, Dongwan Kang, Hwanil Choi, Soonyoung Lee, Wonbin Ahn
First: 2026-10-12T05:49:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T05:49:42+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Time series (TS) anomaly detection (AD) lacks a universal detector, with the best choice determined by the series rather than by the algorithm. Existing methods therefore either select one detector for each series or average a pool of them. However, both assume that no label ever arrives, while a deployed monitor does receive them, since an operator confirms the alarms it raises. In this paper, we propose ONESHOT, which uses a single confirmed anomaly to weight the pool rather than to choose one detector from it. Specifically, we score each detector on the confirmed anomaly, and those scores become the weights of a soft mixture over the whole pool. We show that a single confirmed anomaly is enough to come close to the best detector for the series. Across 152 univariate and 73 multivariate series, ONESHOT outperforms the strongest single detector, averaging the whole pool, and even picking the best detector on the confirmed event. Code is available at: https://github.com/seunghan96/one_shot.

CHAIN: Calibrated LLM Forecasting via Causal-Temporal Hypergraph Inference
Authors: Wenjin Liu, Chenxi Wang, Yue Lu, Zhe Cui, Haoran Luo
First: 2026-09-29T04:30:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T01:19:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large language models have achieved significant progress in event forecasting, yet their probability outputs exhibit systematic calibration bias that varies heterogeneously across different domains and question types, undermining the trustworthiness of probabilistic outputs for decision-making under uncertainty. However, existing calibration methods typically correct probability outputs after prediction is complete, without modeling the structural sources of bias within the prediction process itself. To address this challenge, we decompose probabilistic prediction over causal-temporal hypergraphs into three stages, evidence weighting, evidence aggregation, and source fusion, and propose CHAIN, which designs stage-specific mechanisms to mitigate bias at each stage: (i) modulating the temporal decay function by causal topological distance, (ii) aggregating approximately independent causal chains via Noisy-OR after direction-aware deduplication, and (iii) driving adaptive fusion by causal coverage and directional balance. Experimental results on cross-domain forecasting benchmarks show CHAIN outperforms existing methods in expected calibration error, Brier score, and accuracy. Our project is available at https://github.com/QwenQKing/Chain.

Abstract
A topological group, defined in terms of ``composable minimal geodesics'' is constructed and shown to be homotopy equivalent to the pointed loop space of a path-connected, simply connected Riemannian manifold $M$ of positive convexity radius. This construction is analogous to John Milnor's free group model for the pointed loop space of a connected simplicial complex. Related models built from composable minimal geodesics are also obtained for the free loop space of $M$ and for the space of maps from any finite two-dimensional CW-complex into $M$.

Authors: Keshu Wu, Sixu Li, Zihao Li, Zhiwen Fan, Xiaopeng Li, Yang Zhou
First: 2026-05-17T08:15:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-11T02:28:48+00:00

Abstract
Traffic sensor data exhibit strong temporal and cross-sensor dependencies, yet long gaps erase both a sensor's local history and the joint observations needed to learn its relationships with other sensors. We introduce MoHL, a mixture of hypergraph Laplacians that learns group-wise relational structure directly from incomplete spatiotemporal data. MoHL discovers overlapping sensor groups from observed co-variation, constructs a Laplacian component for each interaction order, and learns their mixture through holdouts that mimic the missingness pattern. By sharing weights across group-induced couplings, MoHL pools scattered evidence instead of estimating each coupling independently. We characterize the induced weight-sharing structure and analyze the conditions under which sharing reduces estimation risk. Experiments on three traffic networks show that higher-order groups are most beneficial under long gaps, while support-matched comparisons demonstrate the advantage of shared weights when relational evidence is scarce. MoHL achieves the lowest error among the evaluated baseline methods under day-long gaps, with orders of magnitude fewer learned parameters than deep imputers. These results show how group-wise sharing can sustain reliable reconstruction precisely when missingness depletes the evidence needed to learn cross-sensor dependence.

Correlation-Aware Reconstruction for Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Md. Johir Raihan, Zahra Zamanzadeh Darban, Yueyang Liu, Flora D. Salim, Levin Kuhlmann, Mahsa Salehi
First: 2026-10-11T00:46:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-11T00:46:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reconstruction-based detectors for multivariate time series anomaly detection typically learn a single entangled representation of normal behavior and flag anomalies based on reconstruction error. However, real-world anomalies are not only isolated spikes in a single channel but also disruptions in the relationships between channels, where each sensor individually appears normal, but their joint behavior deviates from the norm. Because these models compress both temporal evolution and inter-channel structure into this single space, they lack an explicit mechanism to isolate such inter-channel dependencies from normal temporal dependencies, leading to lower detection rates for cross-channel anomalies. We propose CARMA (Correlation-Aware Reconstruction for Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection), which is a reconstruction-based variational autoencoder with two complementary latent spaces: a temporal latent that captures sequential dynamics, and a correlation latent that is trained to reconstruct the window's pairwise Pearson correlations. Both representations are fused in the signal decoder, and anomalies are detected from the reconstruction error. We evaluate CARMA on the TSB-AD-M benchmark suite (17 datasets, 180 series) against 29 baseline methods, and show that it significantly outperforms all of them across multiple threshold-free and range-aware metrics, achieving a VUS-PR of 0.4929. Ablations show that removing the correlation branch degrades performance, that adding a second latent without supervision recovers only part of the gain, and that supervising the correlation latent with an explicit correlation target accounts for the rest and produces a sharper error response at anomalies.

Reconstruction-based detectors for multivariate time series anomaly detection typically learn a single entangled representation of normal behavior and flag anomalies based on reconstruction error.
Topological realizations of finite posets
Authors: Vin de Silva
First: 2026-10-10T18:16:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-10T18:16:14+00:00
Comments: 34 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
I introduce a new class of cell complexes, called CL-complexes, that provide alternative topological realizations of finite posets. Each realization of a given poset has the same homotopy type as the order complex of that, so in some sense these spaces do not provide anything really new. Their value is that they enable short transparent proofs of standard results in poset homotopy. These include Quillen's Theorem A for finite posets, the Nerve Theorem for good covers of a topological space, and Sheehy's multicover generalization of the Nerve Theorem. Order complexes retain a special status: the order complex of a finite poset admits CL-complex structures both for the poset itself and for its opposite poset. The definition of CL-complexes is motivated by an elementary generalization of the Carrier Lemma of J. W. Walker, which in its original form constructs a canonical homotopy class of maps from a simplicial complex to a space covered by a suitable family of contractible sets. In the generalized lemma, the role played by simplicial complexes and the role played by spaces covered by contractible sets can both be fulfilled by CL-complexes. This sets up an equivalence of categories that tacitly underlies our approach.

I introduce a new class of cell complexes, called CL-complexes, that provide alternative topological realizations of finite posets.
