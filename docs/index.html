Tracking a Small Set of Experts in Online Convex Optimization
Authors: Angelos Assos, Anand Brahmbhatt, Elad Hazan
First: 2026-10-14T16:13:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:13:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
For the fundamental problem of prediction from expert advice, Freund asked whether one could compete with a comparator that switches $k$ times among only $m$ experts. For a discrete set of experts, this problem was resolved by Bousquet and Warmuth (2020), who gave an efficient algorithm with optimal regret matching the information-theoretic limit. We study the analogous problem in online convex optimization, where the action space is continuous. An inefficient algorithm can be derived by discretizing the action space and applying the Bousquet--Warmuth algorithm, giving regret of $\widetilde{O}(md+k)$, while the best known efficient algorithm for this problem has regret $\widetilde{O}(dk)$. We show that this computational inefficiency is inherent, establishing a separation between the information-theoretic and polynomial-time regret achievable for this problem. Specifically, under the hypergraphic planted-clique conjecture, we show that for every polynomial-time algorithm there exist instances for which it incurs at least $(k\sqrt d)^{1-o(1)}$ regret.
From Human Corrections to Learned Segmentations: A Critical Mathematical Review of Information, Learning, and Human Effort
Authors: Jon Sporring
First: 2026-10-14T16:00:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:00:20+00:00
Comments: 40 pages, literature-selection and search record included
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
When does a human correction become useful learning, and what establishes better segmentation at matched human effort? This critical narrative review follows feedback through its selection, learner update, and evaluation. It compares active learning, machine teaching, interactive segmentation, and sequential predictive evaluation using a common mathematical account of target information, predictive error, response predictability, and human work. An exact Bayesian update converts evaluation-label information into expected log-loss improvement; an excess-risk decomposition explains what an approximate learner can lose. Conditional information decompositions separate the roles of selected locations, labels, and displayed context. Empirical comparisons show why response protocols, update mechanisms, and effort denominators matter, distinguishing current-mask refinement from reusable learning and simulated interaction from human evidence. We identify what logs can reveal, what requires independent evaluation, and what remains model-dependent. The resulting research priorities are to test what selection reveals, whether using it improves assimilation, and whether the resulting interaction policy benefits segmentation at matched effort. These are distinct claims: predicting a teacher's responses need not identify human intentions or establish better learning. No new acquisition algorithm or empirical superiority is claimed.

Discrete Morse Theory for Computing Filtered Cosheaf Homology
Authors: Neria Baris
First: 2026-10-14T15:45:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T15:45:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper addresses the computation of persistent homology for filtrations of cellular cosheaves over a fixed cell complex, where the algebraic data vary along the filtration while the underlying topological structure remains unchanged. We extend methods from discrete Morse theory to this setting, establishing conditions under which chain complexes can be reduced while preserving the associated persistence module. Building on these results, we introduce "FilteredScythe", an algorithm that identifies Morse matchings compatible with all stages of the filtration and performs reductions using Schur complements, based on the exist "Scythe" algorithm. We prove the correctness of these reductions and establish bounds on the algorithm's time and space complexity.
Topology-Aware Representation Alignment for Semi-Supervised Vision-Language Learning
Authors: Junwon You, Mihyun Jang, Sangwoo Mo, Jae-Hun Jung

Abstract
Vision-language models have shown strong performance, but they often generalize poorly to specialized domains. While semi-supervised vision-language learning mitigates this limitation by leveraging a small set of labeled image-text pairs together with abundant unlabeled images, existing methods remain fundamentally pairwise and fail to model the global structure of multimodal representation manifolds. Most existing topology-based alignment methods rely on persistence diagram matching, which neither guarantees geometric alignment nor uses the image-text pairing information central to vision-language learning. We propose Topology-Aware Representation Alignment for Semi-Supervised Vision-Language Learning (ToMA), a framework that uses persistent homology to decompose pairwise relations into $H_0$-death and $H_1$-birth relations and aligns them across modalities through available cross-modal correspondences. This lightweight decomposition captures both connectivity and cycle-closing structure without constructing 2-simplices. Experiments show clear improvements on remote sensing, while fashion results reveal strong gains in classification and particularly strong retrieval improvements for difficult queries. Additional analysis shows that ToMA provides more consistent gains than alternative topology-based objectives and that lightweight $H_1$-birth edges provide useful higher-order structural signals.
Retrieval Over Training: Similarity search-based Model Selection for Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Christos Panourgias, Roberto Stanzione, Adrien Petralia, Themis Palpanas, Paul Boniol
First: 2026-10-14T14:18:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:18:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Anomaly detection is a fundamental task for time-series analytics, with important implications for the downstream performance of many applications. Despite the large number of anomaly detection methods proposed in the literature, recent benchmark studies have shown that no single detector performs best across highly heterogeneous time series. Therefore, a practical and scalable solution is to develop a model-selection method that, for a given time series, selects the anomaly detector most likely to perform well. Nevertheless, the model selection approaches proposed in the literature suffer from a significant drop in accuracy when applied in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings. In this paper, we tackle the aforementioned limitation and propose RAMSAD, a retrieval-based framework for model selection in time-series anomaly detection. Given a new time series, our method queries a knowledge base of previously observed instances, retrieves the top-k most similar series, and transfers detector recommendations from their performance profiles. The framework can operate with standard similarity measures as well as embedding-based representations. Our results show that RAMSAD outperforms existing baselines in In-Distribution (ID) settings and is the most robust selector in OOD, where, when selecting a small subset of detectors, it matches or exceeds ensembles that run the entire detector pool, at a fraction of their cost. Overall, we demonstrate that similarity-based retrieval constitutes a strong and efficient foundation for model selection in time-series anomaly detection.

Abstract
Anomaly detection in multivariate time series is a central challenge in industrial monitoring, as failures frequently arise from complex temporal dynamics and cross-sensor interactions. While recent deep learning models, including graph neural networks and Transformers, have demonstrated strong empirical performance, most approaches remain primarily correlational and offer limited support for causal interpretation and root-cause localization. This study introduces a causally-constrained probabilistic forecasting framework which is a Causally Guided Transformer (CGT) model for multivariate time-series anomaly detection, integrating an explicit time-lagged causal graph prior with deep sequence modeling. For each target variable, a dedicated forecasting block employs a hard parent mask derived from causal discovery to restrict the main prediction pathway to graph-supported causes, while a latent Gaussian head captures predictive uncertainty. To leverage residual correlational information without compromising the causal representation, a shadow auxiliary path with stop-gradient isolation and a safety-gated blending mechanism is incorporated to suppress non-causal contributions when reliability is low. Anomalies are identified using negative log-likelihood scores with adaptive streaming thresholding, and root-cause variables are determined through per-dimension probabilistic attribution and counterfactual clamping. Experiments on the ASD and SMD benchmarks indicate that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art detection performance, with F1-scores of 96.19% on ASD and 95.32% on SMD, and enhances variable-level attribution quality. These findings suggest that causal structural priors can improve both robustness and interpretability in detecting deep anomalies in multivariate sensor systems.
Uncovering higher-order interactions from memory in observed dynamics
Authors: Arthur Rondeau, Alain Barrat, Roland Bouffanais
First: 2026-10-14T12:34:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T12:34:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many complex systems involve group interactions, which can readily be represented by higher-order structures such as hypergraphs. However, when observing a system's dynamical evolution, establishing whether that evolution actually results from higher-order interactions, or whether pairwise interactions suffice to explain the data, remains a challenge. Even in the simple case of a temporal sequence of nodes visited during a diffusion process, such a task is intricate. Indeed, group interactions leave their traces as memory on the observed nodes, while path dependence in a purely pairwise process can mimic that same footprint without any group structure. To resolve this, we propose an inference pipeline based on a hidden Markov model (HMM) that tests, directly from an observed sequence of nodes carrying no group labels, whether higher-order interactions are required to describe the dynamics. The HMM latent states represent higher-order interactions, seen as hyperedges of an unobserved hypergraph, so that their detection becomes a model selection problem between the HMM class and a class of null models without higher-order interactions. We first validate this approach on synthetic node sequences generated from random walks on graphs and hypergraphs, showing that it recovers both the number and the composition of the underlying hyperedges, while correctly rejecting them when the generating process is pairwise. As a second step, we consider a validation based on empirical data, namely empirical trajectories of ball possession in football matches. This application shows that the pipeline goes beyond providing statistical evidence of the presence of higher-order interactions, and yields interpretable underlying structures: the recovered hyperedges map directly onto recognizable tactical team formations.

Abstract
Asynchronous online learning offers temporal flexibility at a structural cost: learning communities tend to fragment rather than cohere. $β_0$, the number of disconnected behavioral clusters from Zigzag Persistent Homology, serves as a cohort-level indicator of this structure. Two questions remained unverified at scale: (1) does apparent $β_0$ convergence reflect genuine behavioral alignment or learner dropout? and (2) do assessment deadlines produce reproducible fragmentation-convergence cycles? Persistence and Topological Complexity
Authors: Facundo Mémoli, Ling Zhou
First: 2025-06-22T03:39:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T03:07:39+00:00
Comments: 49 pages, 4 figures, 4 tables

Abstract
Topological complexity is a homotopy invariant that measures the minimal number of continuous rules required for motion planning in a space. In this work, we introduce persistent analogs of topological complexity and its cohomological lower bound, the zero-divisor-cup-length, for persistent topological spaces, and establish their stability. For Vietoris--Rips filtrations of metric spaces, we show that the erosion distances between these persistent invariants are bounded above by twice the Gromov--Hausdorff distance. We develop metric models of classical topological spaces based on cube boundaries with the $\ell_\infty$ metric and their products and wedges, allowing explicit computations of persistent invariants and their distances. These examples show that, in certain cases, persistent topological complexity and zero-divisor-cup-length distinguish spaces that persistent homology, persistent LS-category, and persistent cup-length fail to distinguish.

Abstract
Explicit dynamic finite element (FE) simulations are widely used for large-deformation analysis but remain expensive for design-space exploration. In explicit FE analysis, nodal kinematics and element-level deformation measures evolve through a coupled node-element update process. An FE-inspired learned simulator can preserve this structure and learn nodal kinematics and elemental deformation on their native domains. Nodal displacement describes geometry evolution, while elemental thinning identifies local deformation and critical regions. Feeding both outputs back during rollout allows each field to inform subsequent updates of the other. This work proposes CAtt-BiGNN, a cross-attention-based bipartite graph neural network for coupled nodal-elemental learning. The bipartite graph encodes FE-guided state organisation and information flow without imposing equilibrium or constitutive constraints. Mesh nodes and elements are represented as distinct graph entities connected by directed node-element edges, enabling prediction of nodal displacement increments and elemental deformation on their native domains. An edge-aware cross-attention processor dynamically updates edge representations and weights messages using the receiving state and evolving local relations. For larger graphs, CAtt-BiUGNN uses separate nodal and elemental downsampling and upsampling to enhance long-range propagation while preserving their interaction across graph levels. The method is evaluated on dome-shaped cold-forming and corner-shaped hot-forming benchmarks. Comparisons with node-centred baselines, an FE-inspired hypergraph model, a Physics-Attention-based neural operator, a graph Transformer, and attention ablations show improved joint accuracy in nodal displacement and elemental thinning prediction during autoregressive rollout.

Abstract
Persistent homology summarizes the topological evolution of a graph as persistence diagrams (PDs). Generating graphs whose topology matches a target descriptor is increasingly important, yet existing topology-aware generative models rely on soft regularization, producing graphs whose PDs may only be approximately similar. We initiate a principled investigation of the generation problem: produce graphs that realize a target PD exactly. We propose two approaches guided by the implicit local and global constraints a target PD imposes. The local approach builds degree-based PD-equivalent graphs iteratively, and we prove that every output realizes the target PD and that every degree-based PD-equivalent graph is reachable. However, as shown thereafter, local generation necessarily trades off between sample diversity and reaching infeasible states. We therefore recast generation as a global constraint satisfaction problem with a complete encoding solvable by modern constraint programming solvers. This is applicable to any vertex-based permutation equivariant filtration scheme and is minimal under the degree filtration. Experiments on four standard graph generation benchmarks confirm that both methods realize the target PD exactly. Authors: Keunsu Kim, Suyoung Choi, Meiyan Kang

Abstract
Impact-echo testing is a nondestructive testing method used to detect internal defects in concrete structures. However, boundary reflections, structured interference, and measurement noise can produce complex Fourier amplitude spectra, making defect classification difficult. We constructed a two-dimensional frequency--amplitude persistence image (FA-PI) from the Fourier amplitude spectrum. Using a fixed parameterization of exact multi-parameter persistent homology, each retained Fourier component is represented with frequency-bin location as one coordinate and amplitude as the other, and Gaussian kernels are then applied in this two-dimensional space. This construction has a direct analytical connection to the corresponding one-dimensional Gaussian-smoothed Fourier amplitude spectrum. FA-PI was evaluated on an experimental backfill-void dataset and a public bridge-deck impact-echo dataset, and compared with raw and Gaussian-smoothed Fourier amplitude spectra using a linear classifier with a Softmax output and identical signal-level data splits. The evaluation included original signals and signals corrupted by Gaussian noise, sinusoidal interference, or mixed noise, with matching noise types and signal-to-noise ratios during training, validation, and testing. Performance was assessed using accuracy and macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score. Across the two datasets, FA-PI consistently outperformed the raw and Gaussian-smoothed Fourier baselines in the noisy-condition summaries. These findings indicate that representing Fourier spectral components in a two-dimensional frequency--amplitude space can improve impact-echo defect classification beyond frequency-axis smoothing alone.

Abstract
Training data attribution (TDA) should enable generative model interpretability and foster a variety of related downstream tasks. Nonetheless, current TDA approaches lack reliability and robustness, preventing their adoption in real-world setups. In this paper, we take a decisive step towards more reliable and robust TDA for diffusion models. We propose to perform TDA with mirrored unlearning and noise-consistent skew (MUCS). The idea is to fine-tune a second model with bounded mirrored gradient ascent, and to measure the normalized skew of this model with respect to the original one using consistent noise samples. We show that, while being conceptually simple and generic, MUCS systematically outperforms existing methods on three different datasets by a large margin. We additionally study the effect that core design choices have on final performance, and analyze novel aspects regarding the overlap of influential instances across generated items and the potential of ensembling TDA approaches. We believe that our findings may have broader implications for more general unlearning setups, as well as for tasks requiring the comparison of diffusion losses.

Directed percolation in higher-order social dilemmas
Authors: Pablo Villegas, Sandro Meloni
First: 2026-10-13T15:00:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T15:00:41+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, 3 figures, 57 refs and supplementary material
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multiplayer social dilemmas on hypergraphs show a variety of emergent phenomena, which has been attributed to group interactions. Here we test whether pairwise dynamics can reproduce them as well. We derive the mesoscopic Langevin equation of a testbed model and find the operator content of absorbing phase transitions: a directed-percolation line, a tricritical point, and a first-order region whose spinodal is the explosive transition. In our analysis, three-player games only set the values of the Landau coefficients. We construct a strictly pairwise model with short-lived memory, in which every event involves at most two entities, that reproduces these coefficients term by term and has exactly the same mean-field fixed points. Simulations confirm that the two dynamics share mean-field critical and tricritical scaling and the size of the critical mass, and that a purely three-player game on a two-dimensional triangular lattice lies in the directed-percolation class. For these games, the explosive transition and the critical mass therefore do not require group interactions, and the critical behavior is that of the pairwise theory.
An Approximate-Master-Equation Formulation of the Watts Threshold Model on Hypergraphs
Authors: Leah A. Keating, Kwang-Il Goh, Mason A. Porter
First: 2025-03-06T02:16:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T08:43:52+00:00
Comments: 25 pages, 10 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In traditional models of behavioral and opinion dynamics on networks, researchers suppose that interactions occur between pairs of individuals. However, in reality, social interactions also occur in sets of three or more individuals. A common way to incorporate such polyadic interactions is to study dynamical processes on hypergraphs, in which interactions can occur between any number of the individuals. A major current effort is to generalize popular dynamical processes from graphs to hypergraphs. One important model is the Watts threshold model (WTM), which describes a simplistic social spreading process and was extended recently by Chen et al. from dyadic networks (i.e., ordinary graphs) to polyadic networks (i.e., hypergraphs). In the present paper, we extend their discrete-time model to continuous time by using approximate master equations (AMEs). By employing AMEs, we are able to very accurately model the mean behavior of the continuous-time system. We reduce the high-dimensional AME system to a system of three coupled ordinary differential equations without any detectable loss of accuracy. This much lower-dimensional system is computationally faster to solve numerically, and it is also easier to interpret. We linearize the reduced AME system and derive a cascade condition, which allows us to determine when a large spreading event occurs. We then examine our reduced AME system on a contact hypergraph of a French primary school and on a hypergraph of computer-science coauthorships. We find that our full and reduced AME systems give reasonably good descriptions of the mean time-dependent fraction of active nodes in the polyadic WTM dynamics on these empirical networks.

Abstract
Persistence diagrams are fundamental descriptors in topological data analysis. Many statistical and machine learning methods require persistence diagrams to be mapped into vector spaces or compared through kernels. Although many persistence diagram vectorizations and persistence kernels have been proposed, there is no general framework for determining which persistence diagram vectorizations are synthesizable. Spectral synthesis asks whether the exponential monomials contained in the translation-invariant variety generated by a function span a dense subspace of that variety. We introduce uniformly discrete spectral synthesis for persistence diagram vectorizations using Fourier analysis on groups of virtual persistence diagrams. We then classify many persistence diagram vectorizations and persistence kernels from the literature by uniformly discrete spectral synthesis.

Abstract
The independence complex of the Kneser graph $\KG(n,k)$ is the simplicial complex of pairwise intersecting families of $n$-subsets of $[2n+k]$; equivalently, it is a Vietoris--Rips complex of the $n$-subsets of $[2n+k]$ under the symmetric-difference metric. Feng and Wang asked whether, for $n=3$, its reduced homology is nontrivial only in dimensions $6$ and $9$, and conjectured the rank in dimension $9$. We answer the question affirmatively over the rationals and determine all the associated Betti numbers. For every $k\ge0$, the only nonzero reduced Betti numbers using rationals of $\Ind(\KG(3,k))$ are \[ β_6=\binom{k+5}{7}+29\binom{k+6}{7} \qquad\text{and}\qquad β_9=\binom{k+6}{6}. \] The proof uses an inductive decomposition based on a simplicial retraction and Barmak's star clusters, Dowker duality together with Jonsson's computation of the homology of complexes of graphs with bounded covering number, and a relative Mayer--Vietoris spectral sequence which reduces the problem to the six-element sets.

Abstract
One-class learning is an effective methodology for time series anomaly detection, yet kernel one-class approaches often compare sequences in a pointwise fashion and ignore the geometry of normal data. In this work, we present a manifold-regularised, large-margin $\ell_p$-support vector data description ($\ell_p$-SVDD) method that analyses sequences as paths via the signature kernel. By incorporating a a graph-Laplacian penalty into the $\ell_p$-SVDD objective function and utilising the representer theorem for manifold regularisation, we illustrate that the dual task retains the functional form of the original one with a modified kernel matrix. As such, existing Frank--Wolfe solvers can be applied unchanged. Furthermore, the signature kernel which is computed through a Goursat partial differential equation and can handle irregularly sampled data and is invariant to reparametrisation has not previously been used for one-class time series anomaly detection to the best of our knowledge. Our experiments on five widely used benchmarks, evaluated without point adjustment, demonstrate that the proposed approach compares very favourably with the state of the art approaches.
The Geometry of the Set of Equivalent Linear Neural Networks
Authors: Jonathan Richard Shewchuk, Sagnik Bhattacharya
First: 2024-04-23T09:20:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-12T09:52:09+00:00
Comments: 135 pages, 25 figures, 7 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We characterize the geometry of the set of all weight vectors for which a fully-connected linear neural network computes the same linear transformation $W$. Said another way, the set of all sequences of matrices (of predetermined sizes) whose product is $W$. This set of weight vectors is an algebraic variety in the Euclidean weight space, called the fiber of $W$ under the matrix multiplication map. Sometimes this variety is a manifold, but usually not. The rank stratification of a fiber is a natural partition of the fiber into smooth manifolds of various dimensions called strata. We derive the dimensions of these strata and characterize how they are connected to each other. They satisfy the frontier condition: if a stratum intersects the closure of another stratum, then the former stratum is a subset of the closure of the latter stratum. This subset relationship can be expressed as a partial order with a single minimal element, a stratum that is included in the closure of every other stratum. Hence the strata can be arranged in a hierarchy represented by a directed acyclic graph that represents that partial order; we give an algorithm that computes that graph. A key result describes the relationship between this partial order and the ranks of certain matrices in the network. Abstract
I introduce a new class of cell complexes, called CL-complexes, that provide alternative topological realizations of finite posets. Abstract
Hypergraph Neural Networks (HNNs), designed to model higher-order relations beyond pairwise interactions, typically rely either on fixed structural weights or on normalised attention.
