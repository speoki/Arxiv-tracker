High-dimensional covariate-assisted ranking
Authors: Yujia Liu, Yuanyuan Lin, Ruijian Han, Yiming Xu
First: 2026-10-19T17:41:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:41:43+00:00
Comments: 48 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Ranking data in modern applications often involves covariate vectors whose dimension grows with the number of objects. Identifying relevant covariates and integrating them with item-specific utilities can yield better predictive performance. In this work, we study a Plackett--Luce model that incorporates both item-specific utilities and high-dimensional covariate effects. Under a sparsity assumption on the covariate coefficients, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for identifiability in terms of the Kruskal rank of the restricted covariate design. We then propose an $\ell_1$-regularized maximum likelihood estimator for the covariate coefficients. Under suitable design and connectivity conditions, we establish the existence and uniqueness of the estimator with probability tending to one, as well as its uniform consistency. With an additional minimum-signal condition, we further establish exact support recovery. Our analysis combines a hypergraph-based chaining technique with a profile-likelihood perturbation analysis, yielding estimation rates that explicitly capture the effects of the comparison hypergraph's connectivity and the underlying sparsity level. Numerical experiments using synthetic data and a horse racing dataset corroborate our theoretical findings.

Abstract
We introduce a clustering coefficient for nondirected and directed hypergraphs, which we call the quad clustering coefficient. We determine the average quad clustering coefficient and its distribution in real-world hypergraphs and compare its value with those of random hypergraphs drawn from the configuration model. We find that real-world hypergraphs exhibit a nonnegligible fraction of nodes with a maximal value of the quad clustering coefficient, while we do not find such nodes in random hypergraphs. Interestingly, these highly clustered nodes can have large degrees and can be incident to hyperedges of large cardinality. Moreover, highly clustered nodes are not observed in an analysis based on the pairwise clustering coefficient of the associated projected graph that has binary interactions, and hence higher order interactions are required to identify nodes with a large quad clustering coefficient.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We address the problem of defining connected components in hypergraphs, which are models for systems with higher-order interactions. For graphs with dyadic interactions, connected components are defined in terms of paths connecting nodes along the graph. However, defining connected components in hypergraphs is a more involved problem, as one needs to consider the higher-order nature of the interactions associated with the hyperedge. Higher-order interactions can be taken into consideration through a logic associated with the hyperedges, two examples being OR-logic and AND-logic; these logical operations can be considered two limiting cases corresponding to non-cooperative and fully cooperative interactions, respectively. In this paper we show how connected components can be defined in hypergraphs with OR or AND logic. While OR-logic and AND-logic provide the same connected components for nondirected hypergraphs, for directed hypergraphs the strongly connected component of AND-logic is a subset of the OR-logic strongly connected component. Interestingly, higher-order interactions change the general topological properties of connected components in directed hypergraphs. Notably, while for directed graphs the strongly connected component is the intersection of its in- and out-component, in hypergraphs with AND-logic the intersection of in- and out-component does not equal the strongly connected component. We develop a theory for the fraction of nodes that are part of the largest connected component and through comparison with real-world data we show that degree-cardinality correlations play a significant role.

Higher-Order Co-Infection on Hypergraphs
Authors: Katrina Liu, Laura P. Schaposnik
First: 2026-10-19T09:31:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T09:31:18+00:00
Comments: 19 pages, 8 images

Abstract
People encounter infections through overlapping groups, such as households, classrooms, and workplaces. Understanding how these shared contacts affect the spread of multiple pathogens is critical to determining what information epidemic models need to retain. We introduce HyCo-SIS (Higher-order Co-infection SIS), a discrete-time SIS model on hypergraphs with explicit reinforcement between overlapping active groups and pairwise transmission. We investigate how this reinforcement affects infection dynamics and whether simplified representations of hypergraphs preserve the resulting behavior. We derive an exact representation of overlap reinforcement as target-dependent four-person interactions and determine when a symmetric representation is possible. Numerical experiments examine reinforcement strength, contact structure, and susceptibility to secondary infection, as well as calibrated pairwise approximations. Our results demonstrate that redistributing overlap weights can substantially change finite-time co-infection even when every individual's total overlap coefficient is preserved. We further derive an exact five-person calculation and demonstrate why equal mean infection pressure does not necessarily produce equal acquisition probabilities. These findings identify how the allocation of higher-order interactions can influence contagion dynamics beyond their aggregate strength. Beyond epidemiology, this work contributes to the statistical physics of spreading processes by examining how the arrangement of many-body interactions shapes collective dynamics, and what information is lost when those interactions are simplified.

Abstract
The persistent homology transform, Betti function transform, and Euler characteristic transform represent a shape with a multiset of persistence diagrams, Betti functions, or Euler characteristic functions, respectively, parameterized by the sphere of directions in the ambient space. In this work, we give the first explicit construction of finite sets of directions discretizing the verbose variants of these transforms and show that such discretizations faithfully represent the underlying shape. Our discretization, while exponential in the dimension of the shape, does not depend on any restrictions on the particular immersion beyond general position, and is stable with respect to various perturbations.

PTOPOFL: Topology-Guided Personalised Federated Learning with Persistent-Homology Client Descriptors
Authors: Kelly L Vomo-Donfack, Adryel Hoszu, Grégory Ginot, Ian Morilla
First: 2026-03-04T17:44:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T08:15:59+00:00
Comments: 32 pages, 6 Figures. Revised version with fine theoretical demonstration on convergence. Also real clinical dataset added
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Clustered and personalised federated learning (FL) needs a signal telling the server which clients should share a model. Most methods derive it from losses or model updates that must be recomputed during training. We study pTopoFL, which derives it once, before training, from a compact persistent-homology (PH) descriptor of each client's feature distribution: the server clusters clients on these descriptors, weights them within clusters, and runs blended clustered local gradient descent, returning one personalised model per cluster. For strongly convex, smooth losses we analyse the complete procedure: for any partition, one round is a contraction with factor (1-eta*mu)^tau; the fixed point is characterised by a local-step drift bound, an exact formula for the effective strength of blending (which yields a calibration rule) and a stochastic floor exposing a bias-variance trade-off of clustering; and personalisation lowers the heterogeneity floor in proportion to a measurable share of the dispersion of client optima. Experiments on synthetic federations, a label-skew benchmark and a real four-hospital dataset confirm the theory and delimit the method. When feature geometry and decision rules co-vary, the PH partition recovers the latent client groups, including differences a linear-subspace descriptor misses, and pTopoFL is competitive with the strongest clustered baselines; on the real federation it lowers the test log-loss of FedAvg from 0.463 to 0.438, on par with the best personalised methods. When heterogeneity is invisible in feature geometry, it offers no advantage over FedAvg. pTopoFL transmits model parameters and is no more private than FedAvg; we give a record-level differential-privacy accounting and show that privatising the descriptor destroys the partition. Code reproduces every number.

Abstract
In general, it is interesting to investigate the complexity of simplicial maps under various topological constraints. In this paper, we consider maps between (triangulated oriented rational homology) manifolds with non-zero Hopf invariant and establish bounds on the simplicial complexity of the fiber of a regular point. The lower bound, which is cubic in the dimension, is proved via a refinement of a theorem of S. Eilenberg. The upper bound is notable in that it is uniform with respect to the Hopf invariant.

LandscapeSHAP: Which Persistent Homology Class Gets the Credit?
Authors: Nikola Milićević
First: 2026-09-25T16:17:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T18:33:53+00:00
Comments: 42 pages, 11 figures, 1 table, fixed typos, fixed errors in Example 3.6, improved stability results, expanded bibliography
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Shapley values, a solution concept from cooperative game theory, have recently become a standard tool for feature credit allocation in machine learning. They provide an axiomatically justified method to fairly distribute a model's prediction among the data features. Shapley values have not yet been applied to explain machine learning models trained on features from topological data analysis. We develop what we believe is the first such approach, focusing on the persistence landscape featurization of persistence diagrams. Because each landscape coordinate is a rank statistic, crediting a model's prediction back to individual persistent homology classes (persistence diagram points) is nontrivial. We introduce LandscapeSHAP, a method for fair credit allocation to persistence diagram points based on a model's prediction. For linear models on persistence landscapes, LandscapeSHAP has a closed form expression that gives the exact Shapley value of every persistence diagram point. In particular, there is no coalition sampling required. We further prove that the four Shapley "fairness" axioms uniquely characterize this credit allocation for any model, not only linear ones. For a general nonlinear model, this unique value can only be calculated exactly from its defining coalition averaging formula, which requires considering all $2^N$ many coalitions, where $N$ is the number of points in the persistence diagram. This is computationally intractable for persistence diagrams of realistic size. We complement the exact linear model result with an efficient Monte Carlo sampling of persistence diagram coalitions. We give convergence rates in terms of number of samples needed to approximate to a desired degree of accuracy. We also prove stability results for the LandscapeSHAP credit allocation, for any model.
Summary / 总结
Shapley values, a solution concept from cooperative game theory, have recently become a standard tool for feature credit allocation in machine learning.
HCFM: Helmholtz Component Flow Matching for Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Shyam Sundar Saravanan, Tie Luo, Sanjay Madria
First: 2026-10-18T06:58:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T06:58:40+00:00
Comments: To appear as a conference paper in ICDE 2027

Abstract
Flow Matching (FM) is an efficient simulation-free approach for training flow-based generative models, making it a promising foundation for scalable time-series anomaly detection (TSAD). However, standard FM learns a monolithic vector field in which nominal transport and local density-changing behavior are entangled. This creates a central challenge for TSAD; local expansion and contraction carry crucial anomaly-sensitive information, yet isolating this signal requires expensive Jacobian-trace estimation over the entire field. We propose \emph{Helmholtz Component Flow Matching} (HCFM), an unsupervised TSAD framework that parameterizes the vector field prediction network with separate transport, compression, and residual heads. The transport head is constrained to be divergence-free, so divergence estimation is restricted to the compression head, while a lightweight residual head absorbs the remaining mismatch. This structured parameterization improves scalability and detection performance by separating volume-preserving nominal transport from density-changing compression, yielding cleaner anomaly signals from distinct deviations of nominal behavior. Experiments on large-scale univariate and multivariate TSAD benchmarks show that HCFM consistently improves detection accuracy while maintaining efficient inference compared with 16 baselines. The code is available at https://github.com/TrustAIoT/HCFM__time-series-anomaly/

Abstract
Adverse weather conditions such as fog, rain, snow, and low illumination introduce structured visibility degradations that severely impair object detectors trained on clear-weather data. When source data are inaccessible due to privacy or trans- mission constraints, source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) becomes necessary. However, existing SFDA methods based on pairwise contrastive learning struggle to capture high-order semantics and often suffer from agreement collapse under severe corruption. We propose REUNION, a hypergraph-guided SFDA framework for object detection in adverse weather conditions. REUNION constructs a contrastive hypergraph over target-domain object proposals, encoding high-order relations through intra-image context, weather-aware grouping, prototype-based semantic anchors, and uncertainty-aware connections between reliable and low-confidence instances. To effectively exploit these structured relations, we introduce a group-wise Hyper-InfoNCE objective that optimizes representations at the hyperedge level, enabling semantic information to propagate from confident proposals to corrupted or low-contrast instances. Experiments with diverse benchmarks demonstrate that REUNION effectively mitigates domain shifts and achieves state-of-the-art performance in SFDA settings.

A Topological Signature of Learning under Noisy Labels
Authors: Zhongtian Sun, Fan Mo, Prayag Tiwari, Kelin Xia
First: 2026-10-16T14:04:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T14:04:35+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
How does noisy supervision change the representations learned by a neural network? Training accuracy measures agreement with training labels but provides little information about representation geometry. We use persistent homology to track loop structure across distance thresholds in neural activations. Experiments span image classification, text classification, and modular arithmetic. On a fixed CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 training probe, equally sized clean-label and corrupted-label subsets show no detectable persistence difference at epoch 10, whereas corrupted-label subsets have higher persistence at epoch 50. The contrast survives uniform-scale controls, even though predictions match the assigned noisy labels for only 7-11% of corrupted probe examples at that checkpoint. Normalisation preserves the crossover between clean and noisy training trajectories on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, whereas CIFAR-100/ResNet-18 shows no crossover under any of the five evaluated constructions. In a separate noise-rate calibration comparison across five pipelines, mean activation norm achieves a higher mean in-sample R2 than raw persistence (0.936 versus 0.908). These findings show that noisy supervision leaves a measurable geometric signature even when agreement with corrupted labels is low. Comparing persistence under scale controls with label-fitting measurements offers a way to study how representations respond to label corruption beyond what training accuracy reveals.

Abstract

Abstract
Anomaly detection is a fundamental task for time-series analytics, with important implications for the downstream performance of many applications. Despite the large number of anomaly detection methods proposed in the literature, recent benchmark studies have shown that no single detector performs best across highly heterogeneous time series. Therefore, a practical and scalable solution is to develop a model-selection method that, for a given time series, selects the anomaly detector most likely to perform well. Nevertheless, the model selection approaches proposed in the literature suffer from a significant drop in accuracy when applied in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) settings. In this paper, we tackle the aforementioned limitation and propose RAMSAD, a retrieval-based framework for model selection in time-series anomaly detection. Given a new time series, our method queries a knowledge base of previously observed instances, retrieves the top-k most similar series, and transfers detector recommendations from their performance profiles. The framework can operate with standard similarity measures as well as embedding-based representations. Our results show that RAMSAD outperforms existing baselines in In-Distribution (ID) settings and is the most robust selector in OOD, where, when selecting a small subset of detectors, it matches or exceeds ensembles that run the entire detector pool, at a fraction of their cost. Overall, we demonstrate that similarity-based retrieval constitutes a strong and efficient foundation for model selection in time-series anomaly detection.

Abstract
Many complex systems combine dense background structure with sparse contextual information. We introduce a diffusion-based framework for analyzing how sparse condition-specific layers reshape diffusion geometry in multilayer hypergraphs. Each layer is represented as a weighted hypergraph, layers are coupled through shared entities, and random walks on the coupled system induce multiscale diffusion distances between nodes. Tracking a Small Set of Experts in Online Convex Optimization
Authors: Angelos Assos, Anand Brahmbhatt, Elad Hazan
First: 2026-10-14T16:13:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:13:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
For the fundamental problem of prediction from expert advice, Freund asked whether one could compete with a comparator that switches $k$ times among only $m$ experts. For a discrete set of experts, this problem was resolved by Bousquet and Warmuth (2020), who gave an efficient algorithm with optimal regret matching the information-theoretic limit. We study the analogous problem in online convex optimization, where the action space is continuous. An inefficient algorithm can be derived by discretizing the action space and applying the Bousquet--Warmuth algorithm, giving regret of $\widetilde{O}(md+k)$, while the best known efficient algorithm for this problem has regret $\widetilde{O}(dk)$. We show that this computational inefficiency is inherent, establishing a separation between the information-theoretic and polynomial-time regret achievable for this problem. Specifically, under the hypergraphic planted-clique conjecture, we show that for every polynomial-time algorithm there exist instances for which it incurs at least $(k\sqrt d)^{1-o(1)}$ regret.

Abstract
When does a human correction become useful learning, and what establishes better segmentation at matched human effort? This critical narrative review follows feedback through its selection, learner update, and evaluation. It compares active learning, machine teaching, interactive segmentation, and sequential predictive evaluation using a common mathematical account of target information, predictive error, response predictability, and human work.
